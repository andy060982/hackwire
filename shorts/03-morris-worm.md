# The Morris Worm — The First Internet Worm (1988)

## Title
A College Student Accidentally Broke the Entire Internet

## Hashtags
#cybersecurity #hacking #hackwire #morrisworm #infosec #internet #history #malware

## Script

On November 2nd, 1988, a 23-year-old Cornell student named Robert Morris released a program onto the internet. He said it was just an experiment.

It crashed ten percent of every computer connected to it.

The Morris Worm exploited vulnerabilities in Unix systems — buffer overflows, weak passwords, and a flaw in sendmail. It was designed to avoid reinfecting machines, but a bad design choice made one in seven copies install regardless.

That rate was way too aggressive. Computers got reinfected over and over until they ground to a halt.

Within 24 hours, 6,000 machines were infected — ten percent of the entire internet. Damage was estimated at 10 million dollars.

Morris became the first person convicted under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. He got probation and a fine.

Today? He's a tenured professor at MIT.
