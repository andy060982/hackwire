## Script

On May 12th, 2017, hospitals across Britain started shutting down. Ambulances were diverted. Surgeries canceled. Doctors locked out of patient records.

It was ransomware called WannaCry — and it spread across 150 countries in hours, hitting 200,000 computers.

What made it terrifying? You didn't need to click anything. It spread automatically using EternalBlue — a Windows exploit developed by the NSA, stolen and leaked by a group called the Shadow Brokers.

Microsoft had released a patch two months earlier. But hundreds of thousands of machines hadn't updated — including most of Britain's National Health Service.

Then a 22-year-old British researcher named Marcus Hutchins found a kill switch. The malware checked an unregistered domain name. He registered it for ten dollars — and it stopped WannaCry from spreading.

A ten dollar domain saved the world from billions more in damage.

WannaCry was attributed to North Korea's Lazarus Group. Total estimated damage — over 4 billion dollars.
