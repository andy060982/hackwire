# The Sony Pictures Hack — When North Korea Attacked Hollywood

## Title
North Korea Hacked a Movie Studio Over a Comedy Film

## Hashtags
#cybersecurity #hacking #hackwire #sony #northkorea #infosec #lazarus #hollywood

## Script

In November 2014, Sony Pictures employees turned on their computers and saw a glowing red skeleton. A group calling themselves the Guardians of Peace had taken over everything.

The reason? A Seth Rogen comedy about assassinating Kim Jong-un. North Korea was furious.

The hackers didn't just encrypt files — they destroyed them. Thousands of computers were bricked. Then they started leaking everything.

Unreleased movies hit piracy sites. Executive emails were published — embarrassing conversations about actors and salary disputes. Social security numbers for 47,000 employees were exposed.

Then came threats of terrorist attacks on theaters showing the film. Major chains pulled it. Sony canceled the release.

The FBI attributed the attack to North Korea — the first time a nation-state launched a destructive cyberattack on a U.S. company over a movie.

After President Obama criticized Sony publicly, they released the film online.

The attackers? The Lazarus Group — the same unit that would later steal $81 million from Bangladesh and unleash WannaCry.

All because of a comedy movie.
